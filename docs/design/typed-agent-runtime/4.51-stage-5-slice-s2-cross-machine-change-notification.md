# 4.51 Stage 5 slice S2: cross-machine change notification ([#2131](https://github.com/llm4s/llm4s/issues/2131))

Part of the [Typed Agent Runtime design](../typed-agent-runtime-design.md), §4. Section numbers (§N.N) refer to that document and its [slice records](../typed-agent-runtime-design.md#slice-records). Stage 5 spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-20-stage-5-production-design.md`, "S2" and "Rules every slice must satisfy".

Slice S2 of [#2129](https://github.com/llm4s/llm4s/issues/2129), on S1 ([4.50](4.50-stage-5-slice-s1-postgresql-checkpointer.md)).
`PostgresCheckpointer.awaitEventsAfter` stops polling: a commit through any store over the same database and schema
wakes the waiting calls of every store, by `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY`, through one listening connection per store. No schema
change and no public type: the store's behaviour changes, its API does not.

## What it adds

- `PostgresCheckpointer` overrides `awaitEventsAfter`; a commit that stores events, and `deleteThread`, send
  `pg_notify(channel, payload)` inside their transaction.
- `ChangeNotifier` (`private[postgres]`): the listening connection, its thread, and the waiters it wakes.
- `CheckpointerContract.wakeBound: Option[FiniteDuration]` (default `None`) and the case "wake a waiting
  awaitEventsAfter within the store's wake bound of a commit through another store over the same storage": one call
  waiting an hour, a commit through a `sibling`, the events back within the bound. `InMemoryCheckpointer` and
  `PostgresCheckpointer` declare one second; SQLite, which polls, skips it.

## Decisions

**The bound the slice names: one second.** While the store's listener is connected, a commit through any store over
the schema wakes a waiting call within a second on a healthy database (the contract case, and
`PostgresChangeNotificationSpec`, measure from the commit's return; locally it is a few milliseconds). Nothing in
PostgreSQL bounds delivery itself, so this is the tested figure, not a guarantee under overload; what is guaranteed is
the polling floor below.

**A wait never depends on the listener** (rule 2). A waiting call registers its wake-up, reads, and then waits on its
own `timeout` only - never on the listening connection, which may be connecting, lost or stalled. So each call returns
within `timeout` plus the bound of the reads it makes (one at the start, one per wake), whatever the listener is doing.
While the listener is down, a call returns empty when its `timeout` passes and the caller's next call reads again: that
is the polling of S1, under the caller's `WatchPolicy`, with nothing extra to configure. When the listener connects (or
reconnects), it wakes every waiting call, so a commit made while it was down - which sent it nothing - is read at
once rather than at the end of the wait.

**A notification is a hint.** A woken call reads the log again (`eventsAfter`) and returns what it finds; if that is
nothing (another thread's payload that collides, a commit that stored nothing past `afterSeq`), it waits on for what is
left of its `timeout`. So a missed, late, repeated or colliding notification changes when a call reads, never what it
returns. The waiter registers before its first read, so a commit landing between the read and the wait is not missed;
wakes that pile up during a read are drained by the next wait (`Semaphore`), as they are covered by the read that
follows.

**NOTIFY in the commit's transaction.** `SELECT pg_notify(?, ?)` is the last statement of the commit that stored events
(and of a delete, so a reader waiting inside the deleted log is told at once). PostgreSQL delivers a notification only
when its transaction commits, and after it does, so a woken reader's new snapshot sees the commit; a refused commit
(stale token, conflict) rolls back and notifies no one (tested on a raw `LISTEN` connection). A commit with a
checkpoint and no events notifies nothing: `awaitEventsAfter` returns events only. Identical notifications within one
transaction are folded by the server; one per commit is all there is anyway.

**The channel and payload** (rule 10). One channel per schema, `llm4s_checkpoint_<schema>`; where that passes
PostgreSQL's 63-byte identifier limit, `llm4s_checkpoint.` and a 64-bit hash of the schema (no schema name has a `.`,
so the two forms never meet). Two schemas in one database never wake each other. The payload is the first 64 bits of
the SHA-256 of the thread id, in hex: fixed in size (well under `NOTIFY`'s 8000 bytes, whatever the id), and carrying
nothing of the thread - not its id, not its content. Waiters are indexed by that hash. A per-thread channel was
rejected: a store would `LISTEN`/`UNLISTEN` on its one connection as waiters come and go, and a channel name would
carry the thread id.

**One dedicated, non-pooled connection per store, opened lazily.** The listener starts with the store's first
`awaitEventsAfter` and lives until `close`, on a daemon platform thread
(`llm4s-postgres-checkpointer-listener-<schema>`). A store that never watches - a writer, a tool - opens none, and the
contract suite's many short-lived stores do not each hold one. Over a HikariCP pool with a JDBC URL (every pool `open`
builds, and the usual caller-supplied one), it connects with the driver directly, using the pool's URL, credentials and
data-source properties, `ApplicationName` `llm4s-agent-checkpoint-postgres-listener`, and the store's bounds
(`connectTimeout`/`loginTimeout` from `connectionTimeout`, `socketTimeout` past twice `callTimeout`). Over any other
`DataSource` there is no other way to connect, so it takes one connection from it and holds it, which the guide says.

**Finding a lost connection.** A connection the server ends (`pg_terminate_backend`, a restart) fails the driver's
`getNotifications` at once. One the network loses silently would not, so the listener waits for notifications at most
`callTimeout` at a time, and after a quiet interval runs `SELECT 1` under the store's call watchdog (S1's `Watch`): a
heartbeat that does not return within `callTimeout` aborts the connection. So a silently lost connection is found
within about twice `callTimeout`; meanwhile waiters poll, so nothing waits longer for it. A lost connection is aborted
and reopened with a backoff from 100 ms doubling to `callTimeout`. No configuration was added (S5 owns the store's
configuration): the heartbeat and the backoff follow `callTimeout`.

**Reads per subscription stay one per `pollInterval`.** Each `awaitEventsAfter` still begins with a read, and a watch
calls it once per `pollInterval`, so the store's read load is unchanged from S1; what changes is latency. A deployment
with many subscriptions can raise `pollInterval` to cut reads without slowing delivery, since the notification, not the
interval, now decides when a commit is seen; the interval is only the polling floor while the listener is down. A
store-side cache that skipped the first read when no notification had arrived for the thread was considered and left
out: it would make correctness depend on the listener having been up for the whole gap between two calls, which only a
per-thread, per-connection-epoch record could show.

**Interrupts and close.** The wait is a `Semaphore.tryAcquire`, so an interrupt ends it with `CancelledError` and the
flag set again, as the SPI requires; S1's handling covers the reads. `close` stops the listener, aborts its connection
and wakes every waiting call, which reads again and gets the closed store's `ProcessingError`.

## Tests

- `modules/agent-checkpoint-postgres`, `ChangeNotifierSpec` (7, no server): the channel (readable, hashed when long)
  and payload (16 hex digits, nothing of the id); waiters woken by their thread's payload only, a wake before the wait
  kept, piled-up wakes ending one wait, a closed waiter never woken; an interrupted wait; a listener that cannot connect
  retrying while waiters poll; a fake connection's notification waking its waiter, and the wake-all on listening; a
  connection whose heartbeat stalls silently aborted by the watchdog and replaced.
- `modules/it` (`@Docker`), `PostgresChangeNotificationSpec` (6), two stores - two pools, two listeners - on one schema:
  a waiter on A woken within a second of each of five commits through B, its timeout an hour; 32 waiters over eight
  threads on A served by one listening connection; a commit on another thread neither returning a wait early nor
  returning anything; the notification sent only on commit, its payload the hash (a refused commit and a claim send
  none, a delete sends one); the listening connection killed with `pg_terminate_backend`: a wait made meanwhile keeps
  its bound, a commit made meanwhile reaches a watch, the store listens again on a new connection and wakes a waiter
  within a second; `close` ending a waiting call and closing the listening connection.
- `PostgresCheckpointerContractSpec` runs the new wake case (83 cases); the in-memory store runs it too.

## Left for later slices

S5 may expose the heartbeat and backoff in `PostgresCheckpointerConfig` if a deployment needs them apart from
`callTimeout`. W2's workers can wait on the same notifier for queue entries rather than polling, if W1's queue
notifies its own channel.
